\begin{abstract}
Curriculum-specific question answering requires more than fluent generation:
retrieved passages must match the syllabus, unsupported answers should be
withheld, and citations must genuinely support the generated claims. We
present \textit{StudyMate}, a retrieval-augmented teaching assistant for
Karnataka Pre-University and CBSE learners, combining board-conditioned
answer formatting, hybrid retrieval, a corrective retrieval loop, an evidence
floor and post-generation grounding audits. Evaluation uses a 174-item
curriculum benchmark: 150 answerable board-paper questions and 24
out-of-syllabus questions. Five findings characterise the implementation.
First, dense similarity can retrieve irrelevant front matter despite high
ranking scores. Second, a small corrective-retrieval grader can reject useful
evidence: in a 40-question ablation it falsely abstained on 11 items, while a
stronger grader matched the plain baseline's abstention recall at roughly 29
percent greater latency. Third, a similarity-gated evidence floor reduced
false abstentions from 8 of 40 to 2 of 40 and raised abstention precision from
7 of 15 to 7 of 9 in a separate paired run; the threshold was selected on the
same benchmark, so these are exploratory results. Fourth, an audit of 300
generated statements found 78 percent traceable to provided evidence, but only
6.5 percent citing the supporting passage. Fifth, two judges scoring 22
identical answers showed unstable faithfulness estimates, with a near-zero
correlation; persona-style consistency was also low on nine activated
examples, between 0.22 and 0.33. These findings favour measurable retrieval,
abstention and attribution safeguards over unverified claims of teaching
effectiveness. Answer correctness and learner outcomes remain unmeasured
pending expert and participant evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%------------------------------------------------
Students preparing for board examinations leaf through old question papers by
hand guessing which topics recur, queue outside a staff room where one lecturer
is stretched across a hundred learners, and fall back on a general chatbot that
has never seen their syllabus. None of these routes scales~\cite{lauber2025}.
One-to-one tutoring has long motivated research into scalable learning support,
although Bloom's ``two sigma'' observation should not be taken as a performance
prediction for AI tutors~\cite{bloom1984,vanlehn2011}. The catch is well known
--- a model leaning on pre-trained weights states things that sound right but
drift from the course material~\cite{lewis2020} --- and Retrieval-Augmented
Generation (RAG) was introduced to fix exactly that.

This paper reports what happened when we built such a system and then measured
it. Five findings shape the contribution, including limitations and a targeted
repair to the corrective retrieval pipeline.
\textbf{Retrieval can succeed by its own metric and fail at the task}: asking
for practice-paper material returned the textbook's preface, because front
matter carries the subject name more densely than any chapter, and the
generated paper asked students to ``list five chapter titles''
(§\ref{sec:floor}). \textbf{A corrective loop does not automatically fix it}:
a $3$B grader implementing the grade--rewrite--abstain loop of Corrective
RAG~\cite{yan2024crag} refuses $11$ of $40$ answerable questions, and a strong
grader recovers only to match the plain baseline at $29\%$ higher latency
(§\ref{sec:ablation}). \textbf{The harm is repairable without a stronger
grader}: the small grader rejects every passage, starving the generator into
answering from its weights, so restoring passages the index already places
plainly on topic recovers most of what a strong grader buys
(§\ref{sec:flooreval}). \textbf{A judged score describes its judge}: two judges
scoring identical answers do not agree ($r = -0.074$ on faithfulness,
$\kappa = 0.182$ on style), so we report the pair and the spread rather than a
headline figure (§\ref{sec:judged}). \textbf{Persona conditioning is bounded by
the encoder, not the design}: the pipeline works end to end for an
English-caption lecture but never fires for a Hindi-caption lecture against an
English corpus, and the five-passage floor suppresses styling rather than
faking it (§\ref{sec:pscval}).

Our contributions are: (i) a $174$-item curriculum QA benchmark from real board
papers with an out-of-syllabus split for abstention; (ii) an ablation of whether
corrective retrieval earns its cost, reported as a negative result with a
reproducible script, plus a diagnosis of why it fails and a cheap repair;
(iii) board-conditioned generation following a named board's mark-allocation
convention; (iv) a grounding audit separating invention, misattribution and
dangling citations, with measured rates; and (v) a two-judge evaluation showing
judged faithfulness is not stable across judges.

\section{Related Work}
%------------------------------------------------
% CUT (8pp): condensed to roughly two-thirds. All 26 citations retained --- the
% breadth of the survey was itself a review point.
We group prior work into strands and state how StudyMate relates to each.
Several capabilities are established independently, while their combined
evaluation in board-specific question answering remains under-explored.

\subsection{RAG Systems for Education}
Lauber \textit{et al.}~\cite{lauber2025} tie RAG chatbot design to formal
pedagogy, defining three assistant roles whose taxonomy informs our prompt
design; they validated on a single German course, ran no acceptance study, and
did not address examination-board conventions. Teixeira \textit{et
al.}~\cite{teixeira2025} benchmarked vector stores and embedding models on
educational workloads, finding MiniLM a good speed--accuracy trade-off and
RAGAS a sensible evaluator, but report a component benchmark without a running
prototype or learners. Neumann \textit{et al.}~\cite{neumann2025} fielded
MoodleBot (GPT-4 + Weaviate) for a German database course, reporting $88\%$
accuracy and TAM scores (PU\,=\,4.46, PEOU\,=\,4.60) across $47$ participants;
their instrument is a template for the user study we have \emph{not} yet run.

\subsection{Retrieval Correction, Fusion, and Attribution}
Our corrective layer follows Yan \textit{et al.}~\cite{yan2024crag}, who grade
retrieved documents and trigger corrective action when evidence is weak. We do
not claim this mechanism as novel; our contribution is the \emph{negative
ablation} in an educational setting, showing the loop's benefit is bounded by
grader capacity and, above that bound, does not beat a plain baseline.
BM25~\cite{robertson2009} and reciprocal rank fusion~\cite{cormack2009} are
likewise standard; what we contribute is a measurement of whether the lexical
arm earns its place on \emph{curriculum} retrieval already metadata-filtered by
subject, level and board. Verifying generated text against evidence is also
established: SelfCheckGPT~\cite{manakul2023} samples the model to detect
hallucination without external resources, and ALCE~\cite{gao2023} evaluates
whether generated citations support their sentences. Our setting is cheaper
because the evidence is fixed by retrieval rather than sampled, so the expensive
entailment step is reserved for claims a free lexical pass has already flagged,
and the student receives the verdict beside the answer
(§\ref{sec:groundingeval}).

\subsection{Structured Retrieval, Delivery, and Evaluation}
KG-RAG~\cite{dong2025} strengthens retrieval with a knowledge graph but carries
a maintenance cost that annual syllabus revision makes hard to sustain; we keep
the knowledge layer as re-indexable text. URAG~\cite{nguyen2025} routes FAQ
queries to keyword matching and hard queries to full RAG, informing our command
dispatcher. RAG-X~\cite{he2025} reports a $30.3\%$ UX gain over $62$ students
but is Chinese-only with no persona layer; ARIA~\cite{luo2026} parses
multimodal engineering material at $97.5\%$ accuracy but does not address
board-specific answer structure. L\'opez-Ostenero \textit{et
al.}~\cite{gielis2025} showed Telegram works as a self-assessment platform
without RAG; Koutsiaki \textit{et al.}~\cite{koutsiaki2025} found
sentence-preserving paragraph boundaries beat fixed windows, a result we adopt;
and Es \textit{et al.}~\cite{es2025} introduced RAGAS as an LLM-as-judge
evaluator. Surveys~\cite{kuhail2023,wollny2021} find most systems
single-purpose and rarely supporting regional languages.

\subsection{Style- and Persona-Conditioned Generation}
Persona-conditioned pedagogical agents predate
RAG~\cite{liu2024persona,liu2025pace}, and embodied digital
teachers~\cite{zhao2024} pursue the same intuition that a familiar presenter
lowers cognitive friction. Existing work personalises toward the student: Liu
\textit{et al.}~\cite{liu2024persona} simulate \emph{students} with cognitive
and non-cognitive profiles, and PACE~\cite{liu2025pace} adapts a mathematics
tutor to a learner's personality. We found no comparable line of work
conditioning the tutor's own delivery on a specific named educator, and
evaluations in this area rely largely on simulated dialogue rather than
classroom outcomes --- a limitation ours shares. Within the systems surveyed we
did not identify transcript-level style conditioning combined with
corpus-grounded retrieval and explicit provenance separation between style and
factual sources. This does not establish absolute novelty.

\subsection{Data Pipeline and Retrieval}
The corpus comprises Pre-University textbooks and board question papers for
physics, chemistry, mathematics and biology across both years, chunked at $500$
tokens with $50$-token overlap and encoded with \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} into a
FAISS inner-product index of $7{,}669$ passages. Curating clean source material
matters as much as the model itself~\cite{roh2021}. Every query is filtered by
subject, level, board and document type before ranking, and passages scoring
below a cosine threshold of $0.30$ are discarded.

\subsection{Lexical Fusion}
\label{sec:hybrid}
% CUT (8pp): motivating paragraph shortened.
A $384$-dimensional sentence embedding is a lossy summary. It generalises, which
is what makes semantic search work, but the same compression blurs the rare
tokens that carry most of the meaning in a science syllabus: \emph{Snell},
\emph{Le Chatelier} and \emph{sigma bond} all sit near their topical
neighbourhood, so a query naming one can rank a passage that merely discusses
the surrounding topic above the passage that defines it.

We therefore add a BM25 arm~\cite{robertson2009} ($k_1=1.5$, $b=0.75$) over the
same chunk metadata and fuse the two rankings by reciprocal rank
fusion~\cite{cormack2009}, $\sum_r 1/(k + \text{rank}_r)$ with $k=60$. Fusing
\emph{positions} rather than scores avoids having to make a cosine of $0.42$
commensurate with a BM25 score of $11.3$. The lexical index is built lazily over
the $7{,}669$ stored passages ($48{,}010$ terms, $0.8$\,s) and rebuilt when the
corpus grows. Because the dense index is flat inner-product, a lexical-only hit
can be given its true cosine by reconstructing its stored vector, so the
downstream similarity floor applies to both arms rather than admitting lexical
hits unscored.

\subsection{Corrective Retrieval Layer}
\label{sec:crag}
Each surviving passage is graded by a language model on a three-point scale
(fully supports / background only / unrelated or front matter). If the best
grade reaches $0.90$ the graded passages are used; otherwise the query is
rewritten into textbook vocabulary, retrieval repeated and the results merged,
and if nothing usable survives the system declines. Passages below $0.50$ are
dropped, and a content-based filter removes publisher front matter before
grading at negligible cost.

\subsubsection{Evidence Floor}
\label{sec:floor}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} attributed the $3$B grader's eleven false refusals to
grader capacity. Instrumenting the loop locates the mechanism more precisely:
the grader does not merely misrank, it returns $0.0$ for \emph{every} retrieved
passage on questions plainly in syllabus, leaving the generator with one passage
or none --- and a generator given almost no evidence does not abstain, it falls
back on parametric memory, the very failure the layer exists to prevent.

% CUT (8pp): restoration-bar rationale compressed; threshold sweep retained in
% full because the chair required the tuning provenance to be stated.
We therefore add a floor. When grading leaves fewer than $n=3$ passages but
retrieval returned candidates the dense index puts plainly on topic, those are
restored in retrieval order. The restoration bar is a higher similarity than the
one admitting a passage for grading, because a harsh grader rejecting relevant
passages and a grader correctly rejecting irrelevant ones must be told apart,
and similarity separates them only partly. Over the benchmark the top-1 cosine
for an answerable question has median $0.602$ (range $0.234$--$0.957$) against
$0.306$ ($0.201$--$0.650$) out of syllabus: the distributions overlap and no
threshold divides them. Sweeping it trades the errors directly --- at $0.40$,
eleven of $150$ answerable questions fall below and seven of $24$
out-of-syllabus rise above; at the selected $0.45$, seventeen and four; at
$0.50$, twenty-nine and two. Abstention is not bypassed: when nothing clears the
admission threshold there is nothing to restore. The $0.45$ threshold was
selected using this same $174$-item benchmark; its performance is therefore
exploratory and requires testing on an independent held-out set.

\subsection{Board-Conditioned Generation}
\label{sec:diagram}
Interpolating a board name into a prompt conveys nothing, so each board carries
its conventions explicitly. Karnataka Pre-University uses a $1/2/3/5$-mark
structure where five-mark items expect a derivation, labelled diagram or
numerical and marks are awarded per visible step; CBSE uses a stepwise
value-point scheme where each point must appear separately. Answers follow the
shape the marking scheme rewards --- direct answer, explanation in textbook
terminology, governing formula with symbols and SI units, then a worked example
--- and cite evidence inline, stating which part of a question the retrieved
material does not cover rather than filling it from memory.

% CUT (8pp): Mermaid paragraph reduced to its mechanism and its caveat.
The module also renders a retrieved topic as one of five diagram forms, since
Karnataka five-mark items often ask for a labelled diagram. Each form is pinned
to a single Mermaid grammar, because a grammar chosen per request is one the
validator cannot check. Models write plausible but invalid Mermaid, so the
module parses the output into nodes and edges, discards what it cannot interpret
and \emph{re-emits} the diagram, degrading a malformed label into a cleaned one
rather than a broken render. Nodes come only from retrieved passages, and when
too little structure survives the system abstains. Reliability and pedagogical
quality are both unevaluated (§\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Post-Generation Grounding Audit}
\label{sec:audit}
Every requirement above is advisory, and Section~\ref{sec:faithfulness} shows
the cost: handed correct passages, the $3$B model wrote Snell's law, which was
not among them. A citation cannot be enforced by repeating the instruction, but
it is a \emph{claim} about grounding and can be checked
mechanically~\cite{manakul2023,gao2023}.

The audit separates three failures a student cannot tell apart:
\textbf{unsupported}, matching no retrieved passage; \textbf{miscited},
supported but not by the passage whose number it carries; and
\textbf{dangling}, citing a passage never retrieved. Checkable sentences are
tokenised against the cited passages and the whole retrieved set; token overlap
is a weak proxy both ways, so it decides only \emph{what to examine}, and
flagged claims then go to a model for an entailment verdict in one batched call.
The auditor tokenises differently from the retrieval arm of
Section~\ref{sec:hybrid}: BM25 discards single-character tokens as noise, but
$v$, $u$, $f$ and $n_1$ are exactly what can be wrong in a physics claim ---
under the retrieval tokeniser the lens-formula sentence fell to four content
words and was skipped. Two numbers are reported separately, claims found
anywhere in the evidence and claims citing the passage carrying them, because
collapsing them yields a headline contradicting its own detail. The answer is
never silently rewritten; a caveat is appended and the structured report appears
beside it (Fig.~\ref{fig:r-grounding}).

\subsection{Adaptive Pedagogical Layer}
\label{sec:adaptive}
The adaptation mechanism applies the following deterministic rules, selected
from the learner record:
\begin{equation}
s =
\begin{cases}
\textsf{step\_by\_step} & \text{if } \sigma_t < 50\\
\textsf{worked\_example} & \text{else if } c \geq 2\\
\textsf{socratic} & \text{else if } \sigma_t \geq 80\\
\textsf{concise} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_t$ is the learner's most recent self-reported percentage on topic
$t$ and $c$ is the number of clarification requests detected in the last ten
dialogue turns. We have \emph{not} evaluated whether this rule improves learning
outcomes; that requires the controlled study described in §\ref{sec:future}.

\subsection{Mock Paper Generation}
% CUT (8pp): tightened.
For a $70$-mark paper the blueprint produces $15$ slots --- one $2$-mark, one
$3$-mark and thirteen $5$-mark items --- with one internal choice, assigning a
Bloom level per slot ascending with marks. A structural validator rejects wrong
question counts, blueprint mismatches on identifier, marks or Bloom level,
missing fields, duplicate questions and malformed internal choices, retrying
once before failing. Retrieval is seeded from recurring PYQ topics rather than
the subject name; before this change, every generated question concerned the
textbook's own structure rather than its physics.

\subsection{Persona Engine}
\label{sec:persona}
Fig.~\ref{fig:persona} shows the persona pipeline. Registration fetches
captions, splits them into $300$-token windows, encodes and stores them
encrypted, then samples thirty passages to distil a five-field style descriptor,
cached once. At inference the question is embedded and compared against the
stored passages; styling is applied only if at least five passages clear a
cosine threshold of $0.30$, and the top three are injected as a prefix that is
explicitly marked untrusted, scoped to phrasing, and forbidden from contributing
facts or implying endorsement.

\subsection{Security, Retention, and Data Protection}
\label{sec:security}
% CUT (8pp): tightened; all five mechanisms retained.
Dialogue memory uses the ten most recent turns, while session-scoped retention
concerns how long that dialogue is stored; the two are distinct. The ten most
recent turns are supplied to the model as conversational context, a
prompt-construction limit rather than a retention policy. Stored records are
purged after a configurable period, $30$ days by default, and a student-invoked
endpoint erases the learner record immediately and returns confirmation. Stored
content is encrypted with AES-256-GCM and learner identifiers are HMAC-SHA256
pseudonyms, so records are not keyed by a raw account identifier; teacher
analytics are gated behind a server-held key and return aggregate counts, not
individual transcripts. Telegram bot delivery does not provide end-to-end
encryption: MTProto secures the client--server link, and messages to a bot are
readable by the bot platform. Transport is encrypted in transit, but \emph{not}
end-to-end, and end-to-end encryption is not a system property.

\subsection{Ethical Considerations for Persona Conditioning}
\label{sec:ethics}
The engine stores a derived five-field style descriptor and short caption
excerpts, not redistributed lecture content, and shows those excerpts as
timestamped links back to the original video, so attribution is preserved and
traffic returns to the creator. The prompt forbids claiming to be the educator
or implying endorsement. Even so, imitating an identifiable person's manner of
speaking without permission raises an objection no technical safeguard
dissolves. We therefore recommend restricting registration to educators who have
opted in --- an institution's own faculty registering their own lectures --- and
regard unconsented imitation of a third-party creator as outside the intended
use of this system.

\section{Benchmark}
%------------------------------------------------
We constructed a curriculum QA benchmark of $174$ items: $150$ questions taken
verbatim from Karnataka Pre-University board papers across four subjects and
both years ($20$ per cell, except mathematics at $15$ where symbol-heavy
extraction reduced the yield), and $24$ authored degree-level questions outside
the syllabus that must be refused. Nothing in the answerable split is synthetic.
Gold answers and mark-scheme value points are deliberately left for
subject-teacher annotation: a benchmark whose reference answers were written by
the model family under evaluation would be worthless. The paper therefore
reports \emph{abstention, retrieval economy, citation behaviour and latency},
measurable without gold answers, and not answer correctness, which is not.

\subsection{Lexical Fusion and Exact-Term Recall}
\label{sec:hybrideval}
% CUT (8pp): three paragraphs to one; both cautions retained.
Relevance is not annotated in our benchmark, so we measure the narrow property a
lexical arm is supposed to repair: for each of the $150$ answerable questions we
take its rarest content word by document frequency --- the token a dense encoder
is most likely to blur --- and ask at what rank the first passage containing it
appears in the top five. Dense retrieval alone reaches it for $91$ of $150$
questions at $\mathrm{MRR} = 0.493$; with the lexical arm fused in, $100$ of
$150$ at $0.566$, better on $32$ questions, worse on $3$, identical on $115$ ---
an asymmetry that matters more than the mean, since fusion rarely displaces a
good dense result. Two cautions. This is exact-term recall, not relevance: a
passage can hold the rare token without answering the question, so the gain is
not an answer-quality gain. And the effect is invisible at small scale --- on
fourteen hand-chosen rare-term queries the configurations were
indistinguishable, an artefact of a sample we chose ourselves, recorded here
because spot-checking a few queries will reproduce the null result rather than
the measured one.

assistant into one refusing a quarter of legitimate questions. And on curriculum
QA already filtered by subject, level and board, the corrective machinery is not
where the remaining error lies.

\subsection{Generator Capacity and Faithfulness}
\label{sec:faithfulness}
Holding retrieval fixed, we compared a local $3$B model against a substantially
stronger model on identical retrieved passages. Asked to derive $F = ma$, the
local model produced Snell's law of refraction --- correct physics, but
\emph{absent from the retrieved context}. Given the same five passages, the
stronger model stated that the derivation was not present in the supplied
material and declined that part of the question.

This is the central reliability finding. Retrieval gating and abstention are
enforced in code and hold regardless of model. Everything downstream of ``the
model is now generating'' --- grounding instructions, citation requirements,
partial-coverage admissions --- is advisory, and a small model does not reliably
obey it. An architecture's faithfulness guarantees are therefore bounded by the
instruction-following capacity of its generator, which we believe deserves more
attention than it receives in system papers.

\subsection{Grounding Audit}
\label{sec:groundingeval}
Section~\ref{sec:faithfulness} gave one case; the audit puts a number on it.
Over the same $48$ items the shipped configuration answered $37$ of the $40$
answerable questions, and the audit read back $8.1$ claims per answer, roughly
$300$ checkable statements. Two quantities separate sharply: a mean $0.781$ of
an answer's claims are traceable to the retrieved passages, but only $0.065$
cite the passage carrying them. The model mostly writes from the material it was
given and almost never says which part --- different failures with different
remedies, and either reported alone would mislead. Twenty-eight of the $37$
answers ($76\%$) contain at least one untraceable statement, the measured
frequency of the failure Section~\ref{sec:faithfulness} showed once. No answer
cited a passage never retrieved, which is a consequence of the low citation rate
rather than a success. The audit costs $14.5$\,s per request against $14.7$\,s
without it (Table~\ref{tab:floor}), inside run-to-run variation, because the
lexical pass is free and the entailment pass runs once over only the flagged
claims. Its \emph{accuracy}, as distinct from its cost, is unvalidated
(§\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Judged Answer Quality, and Whether the Judge Can Be Trusted}
\label{sec:judged}
We report RAGAS-style scores and examine their sensitivity to judge selection.
Twenty-two answered benchmark items were scored twice over \emph{identical}
answers, by Qwen2.5-7B --- the generator's own family --- and by Gemini 2.5
Flash from another. Faithfulness follows the RAGAS definition: decompose the
answer into statements, count those inferrable from the retrieved context.
Relevancy is a direct judge rating, not RAGAS's question-regeneration procedure,
and is labelled as such. Table~\ref{tab:judged} gives both columns.

The judges disagree. Across $n=22$ matched answers, their faithfulness scores
correlate at $r = -0.074$ (95\% Fisher-$z$ CI: $-0.48$ to $0.36$) --- a weak and
imprecisely estimated association --- with mean absolute difference $0.288$,
agreeing on only $59\%$ of items to within $0.25$. Much of the gap is
mechanical: Gemini decomposes an answer into $8.1$ statements where Qwen finds
$4.3$, so the denominators differ by a factor of two. The rest is judgement; on
the binary PSC labels agreement is $\kappa = 0.182$, barely above chance. We
draw the conclusion the numbers support rather than the one we wanted:
separating the judge from the generator by model family did not stabilise
measurement in this sample. A faithfulness figure from one judge is a property
of that judge as much as of the system, so we report the pair and the spread
rather than a headline value. This also argues that lexical auditing and
abstention --- mechanisms needing no judge --- deserve more weight than judged
scores in systems of this kind.

\subsection{Persona: Measured Consistency and Cross-Lingual Limitation}
\label{sec:pscval}
\label{sec:personaeval}
The proposed PSC target was $0.78$ but was never pilot-calibrated. We measure
PSC as follows: the judge sees a persona-conditioned answer, three genuine
excerpts from the registered educator and three neutral baseline passages, and
labels the answer style-consistent; PSC is the share judged consistent. Twelve
questions on the registered lecture's topic were used, three from the benchmark
and nine authored, since the board papers hold only three well-formed items on
electric flux. The persona engaged on $n=9$ and was suppressed on three for want
of five matching transcript passages, so the relevance gate behaves as
Section~\ref{sec:persona} describes. PSC among these $n=9$ activated examples is
$0.222$ by the same-family judge and $0.333$ by the cross-family one, both far
below $0.78$: the target is not met. Generating persona and standard answers
from the \emph{same} passages isolates the style prefix; conditioning left
lexical grounding essentially unchanged ($0.943 \rightarrow 0.962$), lowered
judged faithfulness slightly under both judges ($0.690 \rightarrow 0.630$ and
$0.661 \rightarrow 0.638$), moved relevancy in opposite directions depending on
the judge, and lengthened answers by about $11\%$ --- close to quality-neutral,
and short of the proposed target.

The limitation is the encoder. A registered Hindi-caption lecture ($219{,}041$
embedding tokens, truncated to $89{,}991$ to fit the engine's ceiling, yielding
$360$ passages) produced a correct style descriptor identifying Hindi--English
code-switching, Hindi discourse markers and a step-by-step questioning approach,
yet conditioning never fired: with an English-trained encoder an English
question retrieves the English corpus but matches fewer than five Hindi
passages, while a Hindi question fails the course-evidence gate. The two
requirements are mutually exclusive under a monolingual encoder, and the
five-passage floor correctly suppressed styling rather than faking it. An
English-caption lecture ($8{,}444$ tokens, $34$ passages) on the same code path
applied persona on the first attempt, displaying the educator name, the
applied-style notice and timestamped source links under a heading marking them
as teaching-style rather than factual references; every numbered citation
resolved to the institutional textbook, so the provenance separation held. This
establishes that the pipeline operates with style and fact provenance separate.
It does \emph{not} establish that the text is closer to the educator's style.

\section{What Remains Unmeasured}
\label{sec:limits}
%------------------------------------------------
% CUT (8pp): condensed further. All nine open items retained.
Several quantities have been measured; the remainder are listed as open rather
than estimated.

\textbf{Measurement validity.} PSC is measured but its judges disagree: the
$0.78$ target was never pilot-calibrated, and Section~\ref{sec:pscval} reports
$0.222$ and $0.333$ while the judges agree at only $\kappa = 0.182$ --- the
direction is dependable, the value is not, and settling it needs human raters,
an inter-rater statistic and more than twelve questions. LLM-as-judge
circularity is likewise measured, and the news is bad: separating judge from
generator by family was our proposed mitigation, and
Section~\ref{sec:judged} finds the judges fail to agree on identical answers
($r = -0.074$); missing are a human-rated calibration subset, position-swap
debiasing and a larger panel. The grounding audit is itself unvalidated --- we
report how often it flags a claim, not whether it is right to, and neither
false-alarm nor miss rate is known.

\textbf{No human evaluation.} Judged faithfulness measures whether an answer
follows from the retrieved passages, not whether it is correct or would earn
marks; faculty annotation of the benchmark's gold answers is incomplete, so we
report no accuracy figure, and a model judge is not a substitute. The TAM
instrument~\cite{davis1989} and the $N \geq 30$ cohort were planned, not
executed; TAM records what participants report about a system they have used, so
no simulation can stand in for it.

\textbf{Components described but not evaluated.} PYQ topic matching is lexical:
the trend engine ranks single-token keywords by TF-IDF, does not match
differently worded questions expressing one concept, and its extracted topics
include PDF artefacts, with no ranking accuracy verified against a
teacher-labelled key. Practice papers are validated structurally, not
academically --- whether questions meet board standard, whether an assigned
Bloom level matches the question generated, and whether a paper aligns with
learning outcomes are content judgements needing faculty annotation. Speech
coverage is declared, not measured: the interface accepts eight languages ---
English, Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Marathi, Bengali --- with
Whisper (base) transcribing and synthesis disabled by default, but we have
measured word error rate for none, nor the downstream effect of a transcription
error, which matters more since a misheard term changes the retrieval query
itself. Diagram generation is likewise described, not evaluated: we report no
rate at which diagrams are drawn, abstained on or repaired, since re-emission
makes output parseable by construction --- a property of the code, not a
measured result.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}
%------------------------------------------------
The benchmark identifiers, ablation drivers, interface screenshots and
table-generation scripts are available in the public artifact repository
(\url{https://github.com/Monish-Venkat/study-mate}). Board-paper questions and
textbook passages may be subject to third-party copyright: the distributable
artifact includes source identifiers, retrieval and annotation metadata and
reproduction scripts rather than redistributing protected passages without
permission. Each ablation restarts the service in every configuration it reports
and writes both Markdown and \LaTeX{}, so Tables~\ref{tab:ablation}
and~\ref{tab:floor} can be regenerated rather than taken on trust, and the
retrieval study behind the threshold sweep runs without a generator at all. The
judged evaluation caches every answer with the passages that produced it, so a
third judge can re-score identical answers without regenerating them. Because
generation is sampled, the evidence-floor rows are measured against a re-run of
the shipped configuration rather than the published numbers.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:future}
%------------------------------------------------
% CUT (8pp): three paragraphs tightened to two.
We presented StudyMate, a curriculum-grounded teaching assistant for Karnataka
Pre-University and CBSE students, and measured it rather than projecting it.
Retrieval already filtered by subject, level and board is hard to improve with a
corrective loop: a weak grader is actively harmful, a strong one costs latency
without measurably improving grounding, and a near-zero-cost boilerplate filter
matched both. The harm proved specific and cheap to fix --- the grader starves
the generator rather than misranking for it, and a similarity-gated evidence
floor recovers most of what a strong grader buys without running one. Grounding
is likewise better verified than instructed: across $300$ audited statements,
$78\%$ were traceable to the retrieved passages while only $6.5\%$ named the
passage supporting them. Persona conditioning works end to end with correct
provenance separation, but only when the encoder spans the languages involved,
and against the style metric we proposed it reaches $0.22$--$0.33$ rather than
$0.78$. The judged evaluation carries a warning we did not anticipate:
separating judge from generator by model family was our answer to a circularity
objection, and doing it revealed that two judges scoring identical answers do
not agree. The separation removes the objection without producing a stable
number, so we weight the parts of this evaluation that need no judge ---
abstention against a held-out split, lexical audits, retrieval measured directly
--- and suggest that a reported LLM-judged faithfulness figure name its judge
with equal prominence.

Three lines of work follow. A multilingual encoder should restore cross-lingual
persona conditioning, the change most likely to help the Hindi- and
Kannada-medium learners the engine was designed for. Teacher annotation of the
benchmark would convert board-conditioned generation from a design argument into
a measured one --- whether answers written to a board's convention earn more
marks under its scheme, scored blind by practising examiners --- and would
supply the human anchor the judged evaluation lacks. And abstention deserves
first-class treatment, trading against coverage in ways a single accuracy number
hides. Beyond these, federated corpus sharing~\cite{fajardo2025} would let
colleges pool material without centralising student data. We note finally that
the most useful result in this paper began as a negative one: reporting the
corrective layer's failure is what made it legible enough to diagnose, and the
diagnosis is what produced the repair.
